Access Control Policy

Barangay Health System | Version 1.0 | Prepared: 30 October 2026
Owner: Barangay Health Office | Status: Proposed for approval | Effective upon approval

1. Overview and scope

The Barangay Health Office is a community healthcare organization. Its website supports resident registration, family and purok/subgroup records, electronic health cards, diagnoses, vaccinations, medications, referrals, medicine supplies, announcements, and health events. It handles sensitive health information, identifying and contact details, account credentials, operational records, and audit logs.

This policy covers all users, website pages and endpoints, database records, uploaded files, QR links, reports, exports, archives, and backups. Access must follow least privilege, a legitimate work purpose, and denial by default. Role permissions must be combined with record ownership, BHW assignment, and approved feature permissions.

3. Access decision

Request → authenticate when required → check role and feature permission → check record ownership/assignment or active sharing consent → allow minimum necessary action and log; otherwise deny.

Authorization must be checked on the server for every page, API request, download and update, including requests made by changing an identifier or using an old link. Hiding a menu or knowing a QR token is not an authorization check.

Policy requirements describe the intended operating standard; they are not a certification that all controls are implemented. • Page 1 of 2

4. Access control rules and safeguards

Accounts and approvals. Use unique accounts; shared staff logins are prohibited. Admin verifies identity and work assignment before provisioning BHW/citizen access; Super Admin approves administrative roles. Record approver, purpose, scope and expiry for elevated permissions. Remove temporary rights when work ends. Disable departing or compromised accounts immediately, revoke active sessions, and update permissions on reassignment before further access.

Authentication and sessions. Require HTTPS, passwords of at least 12 characters, secure password hashing, and a forced change of temporary/reset passwords. Require multifactor authentication for privileged accounts. Rate-limit repeated login failures and alert on suspicious attempts. Staff sessions must expire after 15 minutes of inactivity. Re-authenticate before role changes, bulk exports, backup downloads or destructive operations. Verify identity before password resets; never disclose existing passwords.

Health records and QR sharing. Citizens may access only records tied to their authenticated citizen ID. BHW access must respect subgroup assignments and feature permissions across searches, QR scanning, reports and exports. A citizen without a subgroup assignment may be managed only within the assigned mother Purok; after subgroup assignment, subgroup-specific permissions apply. Wider viewing does not automatically permit wider editing. Public health sharing requires explicit citizen consent, expires after seven days and is revocable immediately. Explain that anyone holding the QR link may view the shared information while enabled. Limit disclosed fields to the stated purpose, block expired/revoked access, prevent caching and log successful disclosures without recording the token. Revocation cannot recall copies already obtained.

Files, databases and exports. Protect clinical attachments, photos, referral files and exports through authorization checks; do not expose them through unrestricted storage URLs. Staff use approved application functions rather than direct database access. Restrict production database and backup access to specifically authorized technical operators using separate credentials; the application account receives only necessary database privileges. Encrypt backups and sensitive exports, log downloads, use approved storage, and prohibit personal email or cloud transfers. Record the purpose and approving Admin for bulk exports; remove temporary copies within seven days unless continued retention is approved.

Integrity and exceptional access. Archive records rather than routinely delete them. Permanent deletion requires the approved retention schedule, documented authorization and a second authorized approver; routine staff cannot perform it. No user may erase audit evidence. Emergency access must be time-limited, approved by an authorized official, justified in the log and reviewed within one working day. If safe access cannot be granted, use the Health Office's approved offline care procedure.

5. Monitoring, incident handling and review

Control / ownerRequired activity and schedule
Logging
System custodian		Log login successes/failures, denied requests, sensitive record views, changes, exports, QR disclosures, consent changes, account/role changes, and backup/deletion actions. Capture actor or anonymous status, time, action, record identifier, result and source IP. Never log passwords, QR tokens or full clinical content.
Daily monitoring
Admin		Review alerts each working day for repeated failures, cross-assignment attempts, unusual record access, large exports and privilege changes. Escalate high-risk alerts immediately to the custodian and Health Office head; disable suspect access, preserve evidence, investigate and document corrective action.
Monthly audit
Super Admin / reviewer		Sample permissions and sensitive accesses, including exports and public sharing. Review privileged activity through a second authorized official. Protect logs against alteration, restrict log access and retain them for at least 12 months in controlled storage.
Quarterly review
Health Office head		Recertify all staff accounts, subgroup assignments, elevated permissions and inactive accounts. Review this policy at least annually and after an incident, major feature change or significant organizational change. Record revisions, approval and staff notification.
