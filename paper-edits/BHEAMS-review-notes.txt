BHEAMS - EDITORIAL REVIEW NOTES
Edited copy: BHEAMS-edited.docx

WHAT WAS EDITED
Grammar, sentence structure, academic tone, repetition, terminology, headings,
figure/table cross-references, and test-case numbering were revised outside the
protected sections. Method descriptions distinguish stated testing plans from
reported results. Conclusions were limited to what the reported evaluation and
test cases can support. No application code was edited.

PRESERVED
The Statement of the Problem and Objectives sections were preserved in full.
The Review of Related Literature and Studies, its synthesis, and the comparison
of existing/related systems were preserved in full. References were preserved.
The numerical evaluation tables, scale ranges, and recorded test outcomes were
not changed. Images, diagrams, equations, tables, and document styles were kept.
The source file in Documents was not overwritten.

ITEMS REQUIRING AUTHOR VERIFICATION BEFORE SUBMISSION

1. Conflicting standard deviations
The numerical tables and their discussions report different composite SDs:
  Functional Suitability: Table 3 = 0.83; discussion/Table 7 = 0.92.
  Reliability: Table 4 = 0.84; discussion/Table 7 = 0.51.
  Usability: Table 5 = 0.86; discussion/Table 7 = 0.34.
  Security: Table 6 = 1.00; discussion/Table 7 = 0.50.
  Overall: Table 7 = 0.56; discussion = 0.23.
Check the response-level data and calculation method before choosing the final
values. Averaging indicator SDs and calculating the SD of composite respondent
scores are different procedures; identify the procedure actually used. The
conflicting composite values were retained for author verification.

2. Security and Usability labels in Table 7
Table 5 reports Usability with a mean of 3.46, while Table 6 reports Security
with a mean of 3.09. Table 7 instead labels 3.46 as Security and 3.09 as
Usability. Confirm the intended mapping and its associated SDs against the
original responses. The edited discussion explicitly identifies these as the
entries reported in Table 7, rather than silently choosing a replacement.

3. Functional Suitability interpretation
The composite mean of 3.27 is marked GOOD in Table 3 and EXCELLENT in Table 7.
Table 2 places 3.26-4.00 in the Excellent range. Confirm the score and apply
the stated interpretation consistently. Numerical tables were left unchanged.
The detailed discussion now correctly identifies Calendar Management as
Excellent, consistent with its indicator entry in Table 3.

4. Item-level Reliability SD
The dependability-over-time indicator in Table 4 has SD = 0.73; the original
discussion shortened this to 0.7. The edited item-level discussion follows
the table (0.73). This is a transcription alignment, not a recalculation.

5. Evaluation coverage and standard edition
State the exact ISO/IEC 25010 edition used. Align the questionnaire, testing
headings, reported results, and conclusions with the characteristics actually
evaluated. The detailed numerical tables supplied cover four characteristics:
Functional Suitability, Reliability, Usability, and Security. Claims about a
broader set require corresponding evidence. The protected Objectives section
was left unchanged.

6. Respondents and completed procedures
The methodology identifies 5 BHWs, 5 barangay officials, and 20 IT professionals.
Confirm the actual number of completed questionnaires and use that number in
the final statistical analysis. Distinguish the sampling plan from achieved
participation. Supply actual load-test settings and results for the planned
10-30 simultaneous users and 100 dummy records if these tests were completed.
The paper should identify the tool that generated concurrent load and include
measured response times, resource use, and acceptance thresholds where claimed.

7. Roles and access descriptions
The Scope lists four account roles, while the conceptual/use case descriptions
refer to secretary and doctor/nurse actors as well. Confirm whether these are
separate account roles or staff responsibilities assigned to existing roles.
Make the paper and diagram labels agree after that decision. Confirm the
intended privacy and authorization requirements for the public QR health
summary. These implementation questions were not resolved by changing code.

8. Related-system evidence
The protected related-system discussion describes VHISION as likely local or
desktop-based, while the protected comparison table marks Online Access.
The comparison's appointment scheduling terminology should also be reviewed
against the stated limit to vaccinations and community health activities.
No changes were made to this material because it falls within the requested
RRL/related-studies preservation boundary.

9. References and attribution
Trochim (2020) and Ghimire (2022) are cited outside the RRL but do not appear
in the supplied reference list. Confirm complete bibliographic details and
add them only after checking the actual sources. Existing citations and
references were not independently fact-checked or replaced. The inconsistent
expanded names for BHEAMS were normalized to the acronym in edited sections;
confirm the official expansion before using it throughout the final paper.

10. Pagination and final formatting
Text revisions can change page positions. After resolving the items above,
update the table of contents and page numbers in Word and check page breaks,
figure placement, table wrapping, and the readability of diagram labels.
The existing table of contents and protected headings were preserved.
Some headings and figure/table references were corrected where the associated
caption clearly established the intended reference. Test groups were numbered
sequentially, without changing their recorded outcomes.
